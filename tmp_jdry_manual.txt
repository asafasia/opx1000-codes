1 General description
Model JDry-250-QPro 3He - 4He cryogen-free Dilution Refrigerator system is comprised of two
main components ­ the Refrigeration Device (RD) on its own stand, and the Gas Handling System
GHS2 with the turbo pumping station (PS) as seen on the Figure below. The stand is made of
aluminum extrusion and has a 1 inch thick aluminum plate to support the RD positioned on top.

Figure 1 Overall view of the Model JDry-250-QPRO Dilution Refrigerator system

The RD is made as series of consecutive cooling stages of the Insert, with outer can and a series
of radiation shields, concentrically arranged around it like a "Russian doll". The first layer is called
the Outer Vacuum Chamber (OVC), and provides a common vacuum environment for the whole
RD. The OVC is at room temperature and therefore the flanges are sealed with Buna rubber O-
ring. Its upper and lower sections are made from 6061 aluminum alloy by TIG-welding, and are
equipped with 2 lifting handles and 4 lifting lugs each. The OVC flanges also have additional
groove for optional microwave shielding gaskets.

The 1st infrared (IR) radiation shield (the 50 K shield) is made of 110 aluminum and is covered
with about 40 layers of multilayer insulation (MLI). It has an intermediate flange separating the
upper and the lower sections for easy access inside of RD.
The 2nd IR radiation shield (the 3 K shield), the 3rd IR shield (the 1 K or the Still shield) are also
made of aluminum. The last 4th shield (0.01 K shield or mixing chamber shield) is made of OFHC
copper by TIG welding as single units.

screws from above. Then the shields are attached, one by one starting from the innermost, to the
Insert flanges. To ensure light tightness of the shields, they are fitting into steps in the plates, and
each has an individual pump out box with antireflection meander design and black antireflection
carbon epoxy coating inside.

Apiezon grease is used to improve thermal conductivity on all connections above 3 K, including
braids and shields.
Removable Still pumping tube assembly is installed into via NW80 Buna rubber sealed O-rings.
Its bimetal aluminum anchors are attached to the 50 K and the 3 K plates in a light tight manner.
In the working position, the 3" i.d. semiflexible Still pumping line connects the Insert to the PS,
while the 0.25" i.d. flexible Mixture return line from GHS2 has a plastic NW16 electric isolator
on the Insert top flange. This isolator is attached to the bellows sealed manual valve V24, attached
to a NW16 removable feedthrough.
On the other side of the feedthrough, a removable synthetic charcoal trap with ¼" and ½" VCR
connections is installed on the 50 K plate. It is intended for the final mixture cleaning before
entering the refrigerator capillary piping. The 1st stage calibrated Cernox temperature sensor and
the 50 Ohm cartridge heater are attached to the 50 K plate (refer to the wiring diagrams below for
details). This allows heating the trap to somewhat higher temperatures then the 1st stage itself, but
not higher than 320 K.

                                                                                                                   16
After final cleaning in the trap and precooling in 1st stage copper bobbin heat exchanger the mixture
enters the 1/8" stainless steel return capillary, silver soldered to the PT second stage regenerator
tube.

This capillary together with the 2nd stage copper bobbin heat exchanger and the removable copper
mesh filter installed into 3 K plate serve to cool the mixture down to about 3 K and clean any
possible impurities from the stream before entering smallest capillaries in the system, partially
condensing it if pressure is high enough. Further mixture condensation takes place inside the
removable Mixture Condensing Unit (MCU) 0.030" diameter 3.5 m long stainless steel capillary.
The necessary conditions are created by the dynamic overpressure of a few hundred mbar caused
by the Primary flow Impedance (PI) of about 3.04x1010 cc-1 and lower temperature of about 2 K,
created by the cold 3He exiting the Still chamber.

After exiting the Still, liquid mixture is led into the Continuous Heat Exchanger CHE-250 of the
spiral­in-tube type, made of Stainless Steel by silver brazing. It is followed by a section of the
silver powder heat exchanger (SHE-250) just above the Intermediate Cold Plate (ICP). This plate
is attached to the 0.1 K plate made of OFHC copper and serves for heat anchoring of all incoming
wiring and support trusses, using the enthalpy of outgoing mixture.

Below the 0.1 K plate, the returning mixture is entering the remaining sections of SHE-250 before
finally reaching the mixing chamber MC-250. That chamber contains ultrafine silver powder,
attached to its copper bottom. It serves to increase thermal contact to that bottom by overcoming
Kapitsa resistance phenomenon.

After crossing the 3He- 4He phase separation line the diluted phase of the mixture follows the
same path up into the Still chamber, where 3He is being pumped out with the PS, cleaned in the
LN2 trap from air impurities and compressed back into the return line, closing the circulation cycle.

The bottom of the MC is directly clamped to the 0.01 K gold-plated OHFC copper plate. The plate
has a grid of mounting holes to attach DUT from below, as well as its own all welded light tight

                                                                                                                   18
copper shield to shield it from EMF. The inner surface of the shield is covered with a mixture of
carbon powder and Stycast 2850FT epoxy to adsorb any reflecting light, specifically the far
infrared and the microwave radiation.
The core is designed in such way that it can be removed from the Insert if needed. To do so, the
0.01 K plate must be removed first. Then special supports have to be installed to hold the core
together and clamps holding the Still chamber and the ICP removed. Finally, the DR must be
vented with N2 gas, the mini-CF flange and VCR coupling at Still chamber have to be released
and core slowly moved down.

NOTE:
The heater resistance CANNOT be measured more accurately from the top of the insert
when the system is cooled down to below 4 K due to the resistive wires (bronze) in the upper

heater and measure it in liquid helium with a 4-wire dipstick.

To ensure precooling from room temperature to 3 K, where mixture condensing occurs, the
mechanical heat switch assembly is installed. It serves to connect each plate individually to the
copper thermal link, permanently attached to 3 K plate as described below (see Figure 19). The
copper link is hollow in the middle and does not move. It has three sets of anchors, attached to it
with flexible braids. Each set has four spring-loaded feet and can be moved by an actuator. The
actuator is a bronze push-pull rod inside of the copper thermal link, attached to the anchors with
horizontal SS pins. When HS is being engaged, the push-pull rod presses on the anchors and feet

                                                                                                                   24
come into a contact, one by one, with gold-plated copper discs, attached to consecutive plates, thus
providing a thermal connection to the 3 K plate. When HS is being disengaged, the push-pull rod
pulls the anchors to produce about 1 mm gaps between the feet and corresponding anchors.
The actuation is performed with IAI Actuator ERC3-RA6C-I-56P-24-50-NP-S-CN-NM. This
actuator engages the heat switch by pulling up on the rod inside of the system. The control program
then measures the force applied to the actuator and stops when it reaches a preprogramed level. In
the event that there is an issue and the actuator does not feel the preprogramed force it will stop
when it reaches a preprogramed distance determined by internal encoder.

Janis Automated Control Box (JACoB) is assembled inside a Knurr DoubleProRack 19" Enclosure
with Pfeiffer miniature solenoid valves and control electronics. The valve control electronics,
pressure and flow meter read-out as well as the control for the pumps is based on a National
Instruments cRIO (compact Reconfigurable Input Output) platform with a real-time operating
system. CompactRIO controller and I/O modules are housed inside the 4 slot cRIO chassis inside
the box. An NI Touch Panel Human Machine Interface (HMI) PC uses the Windows Embedded
Standard 7 (WES7) operating system, and is placed on the front panel of the JACoB. The HMI
Graphical User Interface (GUI) contains the 4He and the 3He/4He mixture flow diagram with
Valves, Pumps, the Compressor, the Pressure Gauges and the Flow Meter shown on the screen.
To open or close the Valves, the valve "CONTROL" on the flow diagram should be pressed. The
GUI also serves as a gauge indicator, showing pressure and flow in real time. It communicates
with the cRIO controller via Ethernet. All programs including cRIO software and GUI are written
in LabVIEW. For simplicity, the HMI GUI will be referred to as HMI.

9.1.4 Impedance check at room temperature
The values of the impedances installed in this system:

Primary impedance:    2.70 x 1010 cm-3;
Secondary impedance:  3.48 x 109 cm-3;

The following paragraph introduces a pragmatic approach of how to check the mixture condensing
impedance for blockage (even partial blockage), without measuring the absolute value of the
impedance.

The basic idea is to pump out the DR, keep certain (fixed) head pressure of helium gas at the
mixture return side, and monitor the pressure increase rate (measured by G1) inside a fixed volume
at the Still side of the DR-core. The user should keep in mind that the resulting value strongly
depends on the impedance temperature as well as the head pressure value.

10 Interlocks and Safety Protection Features

When the dilution refrigerator system experiences an unusual case, e.g., the cryostat vacuum
suddenly deteriorates due to a leak in the OVC or a leak in the dilution refrigerator itself etc., a
large amount of heat will be transferred to the dilution refrigerator during a short period of time.
In this case, the mixture will come out from the dilution refrigerator and cause a rapid increase of
the Still pressure that in turn will cause a rapid increase of the pressure in the mixture return line.
This type of rapid pressure increase inside the dilution refrigerator system is very dangerous and
can damage the dilution refrigerator, and it must be prevented from happening.

Another case that may cause the pressure increase inside the dilution refrigerator is a power outage.
The pumps will shut down due to the power loss, and the whole cryostat will gradually warm up.
Although the pressure inside the dilution refrigerator will not increase very rapidly, special
measures still must be taken to prevent the buildup of high pressure in the dilution refrigerator.

The following safety features have been implemented in the GHS design for this system.

10.1 Pressure Gauges

Still line pressure G1

    · The program has set the following "Hi" value and a "HiHi" value for G1:
             o Hi = 0.5 mbar
             o HiHi = 3.0 mbar

    · When G1 reaches the Hi value, the background of G1 indicator turns yellow; the event is
         recorded to the log; but no valve states change, no actions or confirmation are needed. G1
         will returns to green color as soon as pressure measured by G1 drops below Hi value.

    · When G1 reaches HiHi value, the G1 indicator background turns red; the event is recorded
         to the log; no valve states change, but the Booster pump is turned OFF by software. G1
         indicator color returns back to yellow when the pressure measured by G1 drops below
         HiHi, and to the green color when the pressure drops below Hi.

Main Pump exhaust pressure G3

    · The program contains the following preset "Hi" value and a "HiHi" value for G3:
             o Hi = 1100 mbar
             o HiHi = 1200 mbar

    · When G3 reaches the Hi value, the G3 background turns yellow; the valves V5 and V2
         close (if they are open); V12 valve opens; the event is recorded to the log. When the
         pressure measured by G3 drops below Hi, G3 background color returns to green; and the
         valve V12 closes; the event is also recorded to the log.

                                                                                                                   77
    · When G3 reaches HiHi value, the G3 background turns red, all valves return to the un-
         energized state (the valves V2 and V4 normally opened, all other solenoid valves are
         normally closed); all pumps stop. When the pressure measured by G3 drops below HiHi,
         the G3 indicator background color returns and green when it drops below Hi.

Return line pressure G4

    · The program has preset the following "Hi" value and a "HiHi" value for G4:
             o Hi = 1500 mbar
             o HiHi = 1700 mbar

    · When G4 reaches the Hi value, the G4 indicator background turns yellow; the event is
         recorded to the log; but no valve changes, and no actions or confirmation is required. G4
         indicator returns to green color as soon as pressure measured by gauge G4 decreases below
         Hi value

    · When G4 reaches HiHi value, the G4 background turns red. G4 returns to yellow when the
         pressure measured by G4 drops below HiHi, and green when it drops below Hi.

NOTE:
The events described above are recorded in the log file only if the praDiagnostics = 1 in the
Janis_HMI.ini file (see Section Error! Reference source not found. Error! Reference source not
found.) in the touch panel PC.

10.2 Main Pump and Booster Pump

    · Booster Pump will stop immediately if the Main Pump is turned off either by interlock
             software or by the user.

10.3 Valves

    · Every solenoid valve has a built-in directional relief valve, which will open up in one
         direction when the differential pressure reached 1.5 bar. These valves are all installed with
         the correct pressure protection directions.

    · CV1 and CV2 are Inline Check Valves. They will open and relieve the mixture to the
         Storage Dump and reduce the pressure difference when the pressure difference reaches
         25 PSI along the arrow direction as, shown in Error! Reference source not found. Error!
         Reference source not found..

10.4 Data Logging and Protection

    · All data recorded by the software is stored in the nonvolatile memory of the NI cRIO
         controller. It can be copied to the hard drive of the touch panel PC or a remote PC remote
         access (for the Remote Access to GHS see section 11.2).

                                                                                                                   78
· The total disk space in cRIO is only about 512Mb, therefore there is a danger that the
    controller can run out of available memory.

· A built-in software safety feature is meant to prevent this from happening: if the available
    disk space drops below the "Disk Alarm Limit" specified in percent of the total disk space
    in the configuration file Janis_RT.ini (see Section Error! Reference source not found.
    Error! Reference source not found.), the data logging will stop, and will not resume until
    there is available disk space.

11.1.1.7 Power Monitoring and Recovery

The power of cRIO RT controller and HMI computer are both maintained by a dedicated
Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS). The RT software contains an independent power monitor
loop. This loop continuously monitors the status of the UPS. Upon detection of an external power
failure, the monitor will start a timer. When the external power is restored, the software sets a new
status of the system depending on the amount of time the power was absent, based on a simple
algorithm. The following two parameters are defined in the algorithm:

PM_TIME_STPL = Short term Power Loss Threshold (in seconds)
PM_TIME_LTPL = Long term Power Loss Threshold (in percent of battery
charge remaining in UPS)

1. Power outage time is shorter than PM_TIME_STPL (default = 10 Seconds)  Do nothing,
    continue normal operation. The status of the valves and the heat switch does not change. If the
    Compressor and the Main Pump were running before the power loss, they will be turned back
    ON after the power is restored. If the Booster Pump was running before the power loss, it will
    not be turned on after the power is restored.

2. Power outage time is longer than PM_TIME_STPL  The software puts the system in the
    Standby Mode: all Pumps and the Compressor are tuned OFF, all solenoid valves are in the
    unenergized state (V2 and V4 are normally open, and all other valves are normally closed)).
    After that the Script Engine queues and executes the "SetIdleState" script.

3. If the external power is restored within the timeframe between PM_TIME_STPL and time
    event of PM_TIME_LTPL (default value = 25%)  Script Engine queues and executes the
    "LTPLScript" script.

4. If the external power is not restored before the PM_TIME_LTPL event occurs  the Script
    Engine queues and executes the "ShutdownScript" script.

Power Recovery Management is enabled as delivered. The state of this option is controlled by a
parameter in the Janis RT.ini file (see Section 12.1.1.2 Janis RT INI File Description)

PM_EN = 1; 1 enables auto power recovery, 0 disables

This setting is user configurable and is read and applied at system power up and after a system
restart.

                                                                                                                   95
There is a script command that will change the enable state for power management during run time
(see Section Error! Reference source not found. Error! Reference source not found.). This
command will take effect immediately upon execution, but it does not modify the INI file. Upon
power up or system restart, the PM_EN state will revert back to the INI file setting.
The following scripts can modified by a User to accomplish a set of desired tasks to be executed
upon a power loss event:

1. The SetIdleState script should be programmed by the User, to place the system in a
    Safe/Idle State where it can safely wait for power to be restored.

2. The LTPLScript should be written by the User to transition the system from the Safe/Idle
    state back to the normal operating state.

3. The ShutdownScript is to perform a user-desired maintenance steps on the system before
    initiating the UPSShutdown script command. The UPSShutdown command will instruct
    the UPS to shut down and power OFF the JACoB. This will shut down all automation functions
    in the system.

not found.), the data logging will stop, the "Start Log" button in HMI will turn red and become
inactive, displaying "Disk Alarm" message. The data logging will not resume until the available
disk space is larger than the "Disk Alarm Limit", meaning there is enough free space in cRIO. To
find out the amount of available free space in cRIO go to the CONFIG Panel of HMI Software and
click on "System Info" button. The available disk space is indicated in the "Avail. Disk Space"
field (see Error! Reference source not found.).

11.3 Application Programming Interface

This section describes the JACoB third party Application Programming Interface (API). The API
is provided to allow a third party to develop an application that can exercise limited access to the
embedded real time automation controller in the Jacob for the purpose of retrieving sensor data
and controlling heaters. Before using API, remote access in the client PC should be set up
according to the Section Error! Reference source not found. of the Manual.

11.3.1 Detail Design Description

The Janis third party API is designed to offer a customer access to measured data and certain
control parameters in the automation cRIO controller without interfering with the existing
proprietary interface for the HMI. The API has its own TCP server on the cRIO. It will respond to
requests made by a TCP client that is connected to the appropriate port on the server. For every
request, the server will respond with one response. The response will be either valid data or an
error code. Every response will contain at least a time stamp and a status code.

A simple client example coded in LabVIEW 2015 is provided. This code demonstrates how to
connect with the server, send a request/command and receive the response. Error handling and
recovery of a dropped connection is left to the client to handle as the application dictates. The
example is provided as a LabVIEW project called Janis API Sample Client.lvproj.

The Syntax for commands using the API is as follows:

Command(param 1 ­ I32,param 2 - double, param 3 - double,...)

Parenthesis must be included. All parameters after the first one will be double. Return value is
always a string.

                                                                                                                 123
The cRIO-9063 does not have an ability to maintain time during reboot or power down. To log the
correct time, the time must be set once the GHS is up and running. The GHS software will
automatically get a time stamp from the JetWay computer within a couple minutes of system start.
Alternately, there are two ways to set the time.

    a) Set the time through the BCO HMI. Clicking the Start Logging button on the HMI Main
         Diagram will set the cRIO clock to the same time as the HMI computer. This method is
         currently DISABLED.

    b) Set the time is through the API syncTime() method. The syncTime Method requires five
         parameters: Year, Month, Day, Hour, and Minute. This method will set the cRIO time to
         the parameters provided in the method.

NOTE:
If the time is set using the API syncTime() method while logging, there may be a noticeable
discontinuity in the HMI Log time stamps.

11.3.2 Client Considerations
The client must follow the sequence shown in the example as a minimum to connect and
communicate with the server. The sequence is Connect, Send, Receive and Close Connection.
Once a connection is established it may be left open while the send receive combination is repeated
as many times as is needed and then the connection is closed when it is no longer needed. For
every send, the client must wait for the response. It is not recommended that commands be sent
faster that every 200 ms.

The server expects that the first four bytes of every command be prefixed by four bytes containing
the size in bytes of the command. It is the responsibility of the client to discover the size of the
command to be sent and append this as a four-byte prefix. The server will append a four-byte
prefix to the response containing the size of the response, not including the four bytes.

The provided example is written in LabVIEW. However, the client can be written in any
programming language. The Server creates a socket and listens on that socket. Any client that
attempts a connection with the appropriate information will be serviced. The information needed
is as follows:

                                                                                                                 124
IP Address   This is the IP address of the cRIO Automation Controller. Reference the Janis
             Control Software User Manual for your system for this information (see also
Port Number  Section Error! Reference source not found. of this Manual)
Timeout      The API Server listens on port number 5559
             Timeout is not required for a connection, but is needed to determine how long
             the client should wait for a response before declaring and error condition. The
             recommended value is at least 1 s.

Currently the maximum number of clients allowed to connect through the API at the same time is
hard coded at 1.

This section describes the Janis Jacob Scripting Language. The Scripting language allows a user
to develop "Smart Scripts" for the purpose of automating some typical routine procedures of
cryostat operation.

The Janis Scripting Capability is built around a parser and execution engine that recognizes and
executes a limited set of commands or language. The language is designed around the operation
of the Jacob Gas Handling System.
The Graphical User Interface (GUI) for the Scripting Engine resides within the Jacob HMI. The
Script controls are located in the lower left corner of the HMI MAIN Panel, and are united by a
"System Control" block.

11.4.4 Defined Variables

The DefineParam command allows the user to define a variable that can be used in a script file.
The value of the named variable can be changed on the fly at any time and immediately influence
the behavior of the script. The Variable can be used in any command in place of a script memory
address. Instead of using an address where a value may be stored, a Variable name in brackets []
may be used. The Variable name must be of the format [variable_name] and must match the name
that was given to the variable in the DefineParam command. The name can be any string containing
letters and numbers and cannot contain spaces or slashes.

Example:

10 SetV5 #500

Can be written in the following way...

5  DefineParam       #100,V5_Perc,50

10 SetV5 [V5_Perc]

When the script is executed, the value 50 be stored at script memory location 100 and the address
100 will be substituted for [V5_Perc]. This will result in a SetV5 50 command at run time.

NOTE:

     1. The memory locations used in DefineParam cannot be used in any other script.
     2. The Variable defined in DefineParam can be used in any script.

It is recommended that you group all your DefineParam Commands together at the beginning of
the script file just after the Manual Mode Script name to make them easy to find and modify if
needed.

The CONFIG tab on the HMI has a Config File Type called Script Params (see Error! Reference
source not found.). Selecting this file type will display all the Variables in the scripts defined by

                                                                                                                 158
the DefineParam Command. This tab allows you to change the parameter value on the fly and
immediately modify script behavior.
The Tab only allows you to modify the value of the Variable. Adding, Removing, Renaming or
Moving a Variable in memory in real time would adversely affect the script execution. Those tasks
must be accomplished by manually modifying the script file (Automation.scr) on the cRIO.
The details about how to modify the Variables are given in Section Error! Reference source not
found. Error! Reference source not found..
